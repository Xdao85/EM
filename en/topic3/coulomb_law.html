Coulomb's Law and Electric Field Intensity

A point charge $q$ sets up an electric field $\mathbf{E}$ in the space around it. A test charge $q'$ placed in that field feels the Coulomb force $\mathbf{F} = q'\mathbf{E}$, whose strength falls off as the inverse square of the distance. The field characterizes the source's influence at a location; the force also depends on the charge placed there. The point-charge model applies when source dimensions are negligible compared with the distance of interest.

Electric Field $\mathrm{V}/\mathrm{m}$

Field of a Point Charge

$$\mathbf{E} = \hat{\mathbf{R}}\, \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon R^2}$$

$\hat{\mathbf{R}} = \mathbf{R}/R$ points from $q$ to the observation point; $R$ is the distance and $\varepsilon$ the permittivity of the medium.

Coulomb Force $\mathrm{N}$

Force on a Test Charge

$$\mathbf{F} = q'\mathbf{E} = \hat{\mathbf{R}}\, \frac{q q'}{4\pi\varepsilon R^2}$$

Like charges repel ($\mathbf{F}$ along $+\hat{\mathbf{R}}$); unlike charges attract. The force on $q$ is equal and opposite.

Medium $\mathrm{F}/\mathrm{m}$

Permittivity $\varepsilon$

$$\varepsilon = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}}\varepsilon_0, \quad \mathbf{D} = \varepsilon\mathbf{E}$$

$\varepsilon_0 \approx 8.854 \times 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{F}/\mathrm{m}$. A dielectric with $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}} > 1$ weakens both $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{F}$ by the factor $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}}$.

Integration over $\phi$

An element $dl = b\,d\phi$ carries $dq = \rho_{\ell} b\, d\phi$ and produces

$$d\mathbf{E} = \frac{\rho_{\ell} b}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{-\hat{\mathbf{r}}\,b + \hat{\mathbf{z}}\,h}{(b^2+h^2)^{3/2}}\, d\phi$$

The $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ terms cancel around the ring, leaving

$$\mathbf{E} = \hat{\mathbf{z}}\,\frac{Q\,h}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 (b^2+h^2)^{3/2}}$$

Charged Disk and the Infinite Sheet

A disk of radius $a$ with uniform $\rho_{\mathrm{s}}$ is a set of rings of radius $r$ and charge $dq = 2\pi\rho_{\mathrm{s}} r\, dr$. Adding the ring fields gives the disk field; letting $a \to \infty$ gives an infinite sheet.

Infinite sheet ($a \to \infty$) $$\mathbf{E} = \pm\hat{\mathbf{z}}\,\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{s}}}{2\varepsilon_0}$$ uniform: independent of the distance $|h|$

Capacitance of Two Conductors

Two conductors carrying $+Q$ and $-Q$, separated by a dielectric, form a capacitor. Each conductor is an equipotential with its charge on the surface; the ratio of charge to voltage depends only on geometry and permittivity. Capacitance measures how much separated charge is stored for a given potential difference. This geometry-only interpretation assumes a linear dielectric and fixed conductor shapes, as in the models below.

Definition

Charge per Volt

$$C = \frac{Q}{V}\quad(\mathrm{F} = \mathrm{C/V})$$

$Q$ denotes the positive charge magnitude on either conductor; their signed charges are opposite. $V = V_{12}$ is the corresponding potential difference, chosen positive for the capacitance ratio.

Boundary Rule

Field at the Conductor

$$E_{\mathrm{n}} = \frac{\rho_{\mathrm{s}}}{\varepsilon},\quad E_{\mathrm{t}} = 0$$

The field leaves the conductor perpendicular to its surface.

General Formula

From the Field

$$C = \frac{\int_S \varepsilon\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{s}}{-\int_l \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l}}$$

Scaling $\mathbf{E}$ scales top and bottom equally, so $C$ depends only on shape, size and $\varepsilon$.

1. Assume charges $\pm Q$ on the conductors.
2. Find $\mathbf{E}$ (Gauss's law, symmetry).
3. Integrate: $V = -\int_l \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l}$ from the $-$ to the $+$ conductor.
4. Divide: $C = Q/V$. $Q$ cancels.

Parallel-Plate Capacitor

Ignoring fringing, $\mathbf{E}$ is uniform: $E = V/d$ and $C = \varepsilon A/d$. Slide a dielectric slab into the gap, either with the battery connected (fixed $V$) or after disconnecting it (fixed $Q$). Compare the two constraints carefully: a connected battery supplies extra charge as capacitance rises; an isolated capacitor keeps its charge while its voltage falls. The different outcomes come from what is held fixed.

Spherical Capacitor and an Isolated Sphere

Concentric spheres of radii $a < b$: $E = Q/(4\pi\varepsilon R^2)$ gives $V = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon}\left(\frac1a - \frac1b\right)$. Let $b \to \infty$ for a single isolated sphere.

$$C = \frac{4\pi\varepsilon\,ab}{b - a}\ \xrightarrow{\,b\to\infty\,}\ 4\pi\varepsilon a$$

Resistance–Capacitance Duality: $RC = \varepsilon/\sigma$

If the dielectric has a small conductivity $\sigma$, current leaks between the conductors. Using $\mathbf{J} = \sigma\mathbf{E}$ in $R = V/I$ and multiplying by $C$, the geometric integrals cancel. This relation assumes the same homogeneous medium for permittivity and conductivity. Leakage is a conduction process; capacitance describes charge storage, so the two properties have different physical roles.

$$RC = \frac{-\int_l \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l}}{\sigma\int_S \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{s}}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon\int_S \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{s}}{-\int_l \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l}} = \frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma}$$

Once $C$ is known for a geometry, its leakage resistance follows immediately: $R = \varepsilon/(\sigma C)$. The product is also the discharge time constant $\tau$ of a charged, isolated capacitor.

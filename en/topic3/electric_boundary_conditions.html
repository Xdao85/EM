Electric Boundary Conditions

Electrostatics

What Happens to $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{D}$ at an Interface?

Medium 1 ($\varepsilon_1$) lies above the boundary and medium 2 ($\varepsilon_2$) below. Split every field into a tangential part (parallel to the surface) and a normal part along $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_2$, the unit normal pointing from medium 2 into medium 1. Two integral laws, applied to vanishingly thin shapes straddling the boundary, give all the rules. The field may change across an interface even when there is no free surface charge. Apply the conditions to the appropriate components and field quantities; continuity of one component does not imply continuity of the whole vector.

Loop
$$\oint_C \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = 0 \Rightarrow E_{1\mathrm{t}} = E_{2\mathrm{t}}$$
Pillbox
$$\oint_S \mathbf{D}\cdot d\mathbf{s} = Q \Rightarrow D_{1\mathrm{n}} - D_{2\mathrm{n}} = \rho_{\mathrm{s}}$$

A long conducting cylinder of radius $a$ in a uniform field $\mathbf{E}_0$ (exact 2-D solution). Inside the conductor $\mathbf{E} = 0$, so outside $E_{\mathrm{t}} = 0$: every field line meets the surface at 90°, ending on induced surface charge $\rho_{\mathrm{s}} = \varepsilon_0 E_{\mathrm{n}}$.

Radius $a$
$$E_{1\mathrm{t}} = 0,\qquad D_{1\mathrm{n}} = \varepsilon_1 E_{1\mathrm{n}} = \rho_{\mathrm{s}}$$

Conductor–Conductor Boundary

Steady current crosses from $(\varepsilon_1, \sigma_1)$ into $(\varepsilon_2, \sigma_2)$. $E_{\mathrm{t}}$ is still continuous and, for steady current, $J_{1\mathrm{n}} = J_{2\mathrm{n}}$. The current lines refract, and a surface charge appears unless $\varepsilon_1/\sigma_1 = \varepsilon_2/\sigma_2$.

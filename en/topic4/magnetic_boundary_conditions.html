Magnetic Boundary Conditions

Magnetostatics · Boundary Conditions

Magnetic Fields at a Material Interface

Maxwell's two magnetostatic equations fix how $\mathbf{B}$ and $\mathbf{H}$ can change across a boundary between media 1 and 2. The unit normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_2$ points from medium 2 into medium 1 (the convention of Fawwaz T. Ulaby and Umberto Ravaioli). Split the field into normal and tangential components before applying the laws. Normal magnetic flux density is continuous; tangential magnetic field intensity can jump when a surface current is present.

1. Normal $\mathbf{B}$

From Gauss's law $\oint\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{s} = 0$

$$B_{1\mathrm{n}} = B_{2\mathrm{n}},\qquad \mu_1H_{1\mathrm{n}} = \mu_2H_{2\mathrm{n}}$$

A pillbox of vanishing height: no magnetic charge, so the flux entering one face leaves through the other.

2. Tangential $\mathbf{H}$

From Ampère's law

$$\hat{\mathbf{n}}_2\times(\mathbf{H}_1 - \mathbf{H}_2) = \mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{s}}$$

A thin rectangular loop encloses only surface current. Without $\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{s}}$ (any medium of finite conductivity), $H_{1\mathrm{t}} = H_{2\mathrm{t}}$.

3. Refraction

Field-line bending

$$\frac{\tan\theta_1}{\tan\theta_2} = \frac{\mu_1}{\mu_2}\quad(\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{s}} = 0)$$

$\theta$ is measured from the normal. Entering a high-$\mu$ medium, lines bend toward the surface; leaving iron into air they emerge almost normal.

Side-by-side summary (in the style of Table 5-2 of Fawwaz T. Ulaby and Umberto Ravaioli).

ComponentElectrostaticsMagnetostaticsReason
Normal$\hat{\mathbf{n}}_2\cdot(\mathbf{D}_1 - \mathbf{D}_2) = \rho_{\mathrm{s}}$$\hat{\mathbf{n}}_2\cdot(\mathbf{B}_1 - \mathbf{B}_2) = 0$Free charge exists; magnetic charge does not ($\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$).
Tangential$\hat{\mathbf{n}}_2\times(\mathbf{E}_1 - \mathbf{E}_2) = 0$$\hat{\mathbf{n}}_2\times(\mathbf{H}_1 - \mathbf{H}_2) = \mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{s}}$Static $\mathbf{E}$ is curl-free; surface current makes $\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{t}}$ jump.

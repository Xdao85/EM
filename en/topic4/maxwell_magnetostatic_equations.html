Maxwell's Magnetostatic Equations

For steady currents the magnetic field obeys two laws. $\mathbf{B}$ has no sources: flux lines have no endpoints and may close or extend to infinity. $\mathbf{H}$ circulates around currents: its line integral around any loop equals the current passing through the loop. With symmetry, Ampère's law gives $\mathbf{H}$ in one line. The circulation law fixes an integral, not every local field value. Symmetry is what lets the integral determine a field magnitude directly; otherwise the law remains valid but a fuller field calculation is needed.

1. Gauss's Law for Magnetism

No monopoles

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \oint_S\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{s} = 0$$

Compare $\oint_S\mathbf{D}\cdot d\mathbf{s} = Q$: there is no magnetic charge to enclose.

2. Ampère's Law

Circulation = current

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \oint_C\mathbf{H}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = I_{\mathrm{enc}}$$

Stokes' theorem links the two forms. The direction of $C$ and the sign of $I$ follow the right-hand rule.

3. Using Symmetry

Pick the contour

$$H\cdot(\text{length of }C) = I_{\mathrm{enc}}$$

When $\mathbf{H}$ is constant and tangent along $C$ (wire, toroid, sheet), the integral collapses to a product.

Thick Wire (Solid or Hollow)

Example 3

Infinite Current Sheet

A sheet in the $x$–$y$ plane carries $\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{s}} = \hat{\mathbf{x}}J_{\mathrm{s}}$ (out of the page here). By symmetry $\mathbf{H}$ is along $\mp\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ and cannot depend on distance, so a rectangle of width $l$ straddling the sheet gives $2Hl = J_{\mathrm{s}}l$. Two sheets with opposite currents confine the field between them, like a solenoid. Drag the rectangle.

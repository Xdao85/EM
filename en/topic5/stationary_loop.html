Stationary Loop in a Time-Varying Magnetic Field

Time-Varying Fields · Stationary Loop

Transformer emf

When a closed loop is held stationary in a time-varying magnetic field $\mathbf{B}(t)$, an emf $V_{\mathrm{emf}}^{\mathrm{tr}}$ appears across its terminals. The changing flux produces a circulating electric field $\mathbf{E}$ that satisfies the differential form of Faraday's law, $\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = -\partial\mathbf{B}/\partial t$. The induced field is nonconservative: its circulation around a closed contour can be nonzero. A single electrostatic scalar potential therefore cannot describe the entire induced field.

1. Transformer emf

$N$-turn loop

$$V_{\mathrm{emf}}^{\mathrm{tr}} = -N\int_S\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t}\cdot d\mathbf{s}$$

The partial derivative $\partial/\partial t$ stresses that the loop is fixed; only $\mathbf{B}$ changes with time.

2. Differential Form

Stokes' theorem

$$\oint_C\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = \int_S(\nabla\times\mathbf{E})\cdot d\mathbf{s}\ \Rightarrow\ \nabla\times\mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$$

A time-varying magnetic field produces a circulating electric field whose field lines are closed.

3. Polarity and Current

Right-hand rule

$$I = \frac{V_{\mathrm{emf}}^{\mathrm{tr}}}{R + R_i}$$

With the thumb along $d\mathbf{s}$, the fingers give the direction of $C$; that direction crosses the gap from the $(+)$ terminal to the $(-)$ terminal. For a good conductor $R_i \ll R$.

Example 6-1

Inductor in a Changing Magnetic Field

An inductor of $N$ circular turns of radius $a$ lies in the $x$–$y$ plane, centred at the origin, and is connected to a resistor $R$; $\mathbf{B} = B_0(\hat{\mathbf{y}}2 + \hat{\mathbf{z}}3)\sin\omega t$. Take $N = 10$, $B_0 = 0.2$ T, $a = 10$ cm, $\omega = 10^3$ rad/s, $R = 1$ k$\Omega$.

(a) Flux

Only $B_z$ threads the loop

$$\Phi = \int_S\mathbf{B}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}}\,ds = 3\pi a^2B_0\sin\omega t$$

The $\hat{\mathbf{y}}2B_0$ component is parallel to the plane of the loop and contributes nothing.

(b) emf

$V_{\mathrm{emf}}^{\mathrm{tr}} = -N\,d\Phi/dt$

$$V_{\mathrm{emf}}^{\mathrm{tr}} = -3\pi N\omega a^2B_0\cos\omega t = -188.5\cos(10^3t)\ \mathrm{V}$$

Amplitude $3\pi(10)(10^3)(0.01)(0.2) = 188.5$ V.

(c) Polarity at $t = 0$

Lenz's law

$$V_{\mathrm{emf}}^{\mathrm{tr}} = V_1 - V_2 = -188.5\ \mathrm{V}$$

At $t = 0$ the flux is increasing; the induced current creates a field that opposes the increase, so terminal 2 is at the higher potential.

(d) Current

Through $R = 10^3\,\Omega$

$$I = \frac{V_2 - V_1}{R} = 0.19\cos(10^3t)\ \mathrm{A}$$

Check it with the sine waveform in Simulation 1: $V$ lags $\Phi$ by $90^\circ$.

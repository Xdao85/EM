Maxwell's Equations for Dynamic Fields

In statics, electric and magnetic fields live separate lives. Once the fields change with time, Faraday's law and the Ampère–Maxwell law couple them: a changing $\mathbf{B}$ produces a curling $\mathbf{E}$, and a changing $\mathbf{D}$ produces a curling $\mathbf{H}$. Together they sustain waves that travel at $1/\sqrt{\mu\varepsilon}$. The speed expression applies to a homogeneous, lossless medium. Conductivity introduces attenuation, and in a lossy or dispersive material the phase velocity requires the appropriate propagation constant.

1. Unchanged Laws

Valid for all fields

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{D} = \rho_{\mathrm{v}},\qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$$

Gauss's laws hold for static and dynamic fields alike.

2. Coupled Laws

Time derivatives appear

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t},\qquad \nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J} + \frac{\partial\mathbf{D}}{\partial t}$$

Each field's curl is driven by the other field's rate of change.

3. Waves

Source-free region

$$\nabla^2\mathbf{E} - \mu\sigma\frac{\partial\mathbf{E}}{\partial t} - \mu\varepsilon\frac{\partial^2\mathbf{E}}{\partial t^2} = 0$$

Taking the curl of Faraday's law and substituting the Ampère–Maxwell law gives a wave equation, damped when $\sigma > 0$.

A Plane Wave in a Lossy Medium

The solution of the wave equation for $\mathbf{E} = \hat{\mathbf{x}}E_x(z, t)$ is $E_x = E_0e^{-\alpha z}\cos(\omega t - \beta z)$ with $\mathbf{H} = \hat{\mathbf{y}}\,(E_0/|\eta_{\mathrm{c}}|)\,e^{-\alpha z}\cos(\omega t - \beta z - \theta_\eta)$. In a lossless medium $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{H}$ are in phase; conductivity introduces attenuation and makes $\mathbf{H}$ lag $\mathbf{E}$. $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}$ points along $+\hat{\mathbf{z}}$, the direction of travel. In a lossy medium the fields need not have the same sign at every instant because of their phase difference. Their time-averaged energy flow is along propagation, although instantaneous energy flow can briefly reverse.

Watching the Curl Equations Leapfrog

Follow a pulse entering a dielectric slab. The electric field $E$ and magnetic field $H$ evolve together: spatial variation of $H$ drives electric-field change, and spatial variation of $E$ drives magnetic-field change. A pulse launched from the left travels at $c$ in vacuum, slows to $c/\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}}}$ in the lossless, nonmagnetic slab, reflects partly at its faces and attenuates when conductivity is present. Concepts

Static and Dynamic Nature of the Equations

Which equations change when fields vary in time?

Invariant laws

Gauss's laws

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{D} = \rho_{\mathrm{v}},\qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$$

Valid for static and dynamic fields. There are never isolated magnetic charges.

Coupled laws

Faraday's and Ampère's laws

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t},\qquad \nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J} + \frac{\partial\mathbf{D}}{\partial t}$$

A changing magnetic field gives rise to an electric field; a changing electric field must be accompanied by a magnetic field. In statics the time derivatives vanish and the two fields decouple.

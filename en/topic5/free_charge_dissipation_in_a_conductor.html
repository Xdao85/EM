Free-Charge Dissipation in a Conductor

A conductor is normally neutral inside: $\rho_{\mathrm{v}} = 0$. If an excess charge is placed in its interior, the field it creates pushes the mobile charges around until neutrality is restored inside and the excess sits on the outer surface. How fast? Combining charge continuity, Ohm's law and Gauss's law gives a simple exponential with a time constant $\tau_{\mathrm{r}} = \varepsilon/\sigma$. This exponential model assumes homogeneous, linear permittivity and conductivity. Charge is redistributed rather than destroyed; disappearance of interior charge is accompanied by accumulation at the surface.

1. Three Equations

Continuity, Ohm, Gauss

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = -\frac{\partial\rho_{\mathrm{v}}}{\partial t},\quad \mathbf{J} = \sigma\mathbf{E},\quad \nabla\cdot\mathbf{E} = \frac{\rho_{\mathrm{v}}}{\varepsilon}$$

Substituting the second and third into the first eliminates $\mathbf{J}$ and $\mathbf{E}$.

2. Decay Law

First-order equation

$$\frac{\partial\rho_{\mathrm{v}}}{\partial t} + \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}\rho_{\mathrm{v}} = 0\ \Rightarrow\ \rho_{\mathrm{v}}(t) = \rho_{\mathrm{vo}}e^{-t/\tau_{\mathrm{r}}}$$

The decay is the same at every interior point; the charge does not "travel" to the surface as a front.

3. Relaxation Time

Material constant

$$\tau_{\mathrm{r}} = \frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma}$$

About $1.5\times10^{-19}$ s for copper: neutrality is restored essentially instantly. For a good insulator it can take hours or days.

Relaxation of an Injected Charge

A ball of charge $Q$ is injected at the centre of a conducting sphere. The interior density decays as $e^{-t/\tau_{\mathrm{r}}}$ everywhere at once, and the same amount appears as surface charge. The total charge is conserved, so the field outside the sphere never changes; only the field inside disappears. Time is shown in units of $\tau_{\mathrm{r}}$, with the real time alongside. Change the material parameters and compare the normalized decay with its physical time. The local continuum model does not represent a literal instantaneous signal across the whole conductor; its extremely short metal time constants should be read within that model's limits.

Displacement Current

Ampère's law $\nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J}$ works for steady currents but contradicts charge conservation when fields change: the divergence of a curl is zero, yet $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = -\partial\rho_{\mathrm{v}}/\partial t$ need not be. Maxwell added the displacement current density $\partial\mathbf{D}/\partial t$, which restores consistency and predicts electromagnetic waves. Displacement current is a field term, not a flow of free charges through an insulating gap. It contributes to magnetic circulation even in vacuum, making the same contour law consistent for different spanning surfaces.

1. Differential Form

Ampère–Maxwell law

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{J} + \frac{\partial\mathbf{D}}{\partial t}$$

Taking the divergence: $0 = \nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} + \partial(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{D})/\partial t = \nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} + \partial\rho_{\mathrm{v}}/\partial t$, which is exactly charge continuity.

2. Integral Form

Stokes' theorem

$$\oint_C\mathbf{H}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = I_{\mathrm{c}} + \int_S\frac{\partial\mathbf{D}}{\partial t}\cdot d\mathbf{s} = I_{\mathrm{c}} + I_{\mathrm{d}}$$

The right side must not depend on which surface $S$ is chosen for the contour $C$.

3. Size

Conductors vs. insulators

$$\frac{|J_{\mathrm{d}}|}{|J_{\mathrm{c}}|} = \frac{\omega\varepsilon}{\sigma}$$

In good metals at ordinary circuit frequencies, conduction current usually dominates. In an ideal insulator with a changing field, displacement current remains even when conduction is zero. Their amplitude ratio, rather than a universal frequency cutoff, determines which can be neglected.

The Capacitor Paradox

An AC source $V_{\mathrm{s}} = V_0\cos\omega t$ charges a parallel-plate capacitor. Take the contour $C$ around the wire. Surface $S_1$ is pierced by the wire and collects the conduction current. Surface $S_2$ bulges between the plates, where no charge crosses; without Maxwell's term it would give $\oint\mathbf{H}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = 0$. With it, the displacement current through $S_2$ equals the conduction current exactly, at every instant. Compare the wire and gap surfaces throughout a cycle, especially when voltage reaches an extremum: the charging current is then zero even though the electric field is large.

Conductor or Dielectric? Comparing $J_{\mathrm{d}}$ with $J_{\mathrm{c}}$

In a material with conductivity $\sigma$ and permittivity $\varepsilon$, the field $E_0\cos\omega t$ drives $J_{\mathrm{c}} = \sigma E$ and $J_{\mathrm{d}} = \varepsilon\,\partial E/\partial t$, 90° apart. Their ratio $\omega\varepsilon/\sigma$ grows with frequency and crosses 1 at $f_{\mathrm{c}} = \sigma/2\pi\varepsilon$: below it the material behaves as a conductor, above it as a dielectric. Compare amplitudes rather than instantaneous values, which can pass through zero at different times. This classification assumes a linear medium with the parameters held fixed at the frequency being examined.

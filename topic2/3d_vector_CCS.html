Vector Transformations

Vector Analysis · Transformations Between Coordinate Systems

One Vector, Three Sets of Components

A vector is a geometric object; its components depend on the base vectors used to describe it. Because $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$, $\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$, $\hat{\mathbf{R}}$, $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ change direction from point to point, a vector's cylindrical or spherical components depend on where the vector is located, even when the vector itself is the same. Each component is simply a dot product with the base vector at that point.

1. Projection
$$A_r = \mathbf{A}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}},\quad A_\phi = \mathbf{A}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$$

Every transformation formula is a dot product with a base vector evaluated at the point of interest.

2. Invariance
$$|\mathbf{A}| = \sqrt{A_x^2 + A_y^2 + A_z^2} = \sqrt{A_R^2 + A_\theta^2 + A_\phi^2}$$

The transformation is a rotation (orthogonal matrix), so the magnitude does not change.

3. Position Vector
$$\overrightarrow{OP} = \hat{\mathbf{r}}\,r + \hat{\mathbf{z}}\,z = \hat{\mathbf{R}}\,R$$

For the position vector evaluated at its own head, $A_\phi = 0$ and $A_\theta = 0$. For any other point they generally are not.

Transformation Matrices

Cartesian ↔ Cylindrical

$$\begin{bmatrix}A_r\\ A_\phi\\ A_z\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\cos\phi & \sin\phi & 0\\ -\sin\phi & \cos\phi & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}A_x\\ A_y\\ A_z\end{bmatrix}$$ $$A_x = A_r\cos\phi - A_\phi\sin\phi,\qquad A_y = A_r\sin\phi + A_\phi\cos\phi$$

Cartesian ↔ Spherical

$$\begin{bmatrix}A_R\\ A_\theta\\ A_\phi\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\sin\theta\cos\phi & \sin\theta\sin\phi & \cos\theta\\ \cos\theta\cos\phi & \cos\theta\sin\phi & -\sin\theta\\ -\sin\phi & \cos\phi & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}A_x\\ A_y\\ A_z\end{bmatrix}$$

The inverse is the transpose, because the rows are orthonormal base vectors.

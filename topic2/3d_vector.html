Magnitude, Direction and the Algebra of Vectors

Electromagnetic quantities such as $\mathbf{E}$, $\mathbf{H}$ and $\mathbf{J}$ are vectors: each has a magnitude and a direction. In Cartesian coordinates a vector is written with the base vectors $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$, $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$, $\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ and three scalar components. Everything else (unit vectors, sums, dot and cross products) follows from those components.

1. Components
$$\mathbf{A} = \hat{\mathbf{x}}A_x + \hat{\mathbf{y}}A_y + \hat{\mathbf{z}}A_z$$

$A_x$, $A_y$, $A_z$ are the projections of $\mathbf{A}$ on the three axes.

2. Unit Vector
$$\hat{\mathbf{a}} = \frac{\mathbf{A}}{A}$$

$A = \sqrt{A_x^2 + A_y^2 + A_z^2}$ is the magnitude.

3. Dot Product
$$\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{B} = AB\cos\theta_{AB}$$

A scalar. Zero when $\mathbf{A} \perp \mathbf{B}$; gives projections and angles.

4. Cross Product
$$\mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{B} = \hat{\mathbf{n}}\,AB\sin\theta_{AB}$$

A vector normal to both, by the right-hand rule. Its magnitude is the parallelogram area.

Vector Algebra: $\mathbf{A} \pm \mathbf{B}$, $\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{B}$, $\mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{B}$

Choose an operation. Addition uses the head-to-tail (parallelogram) rule; the dot product shows the projection of $\mathbf{A}$ on $\mathbf{B}$; the cross product shows the parallelogram whose area is $|\mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{B}|$ and the right-hand-rule sweep from $\mathbf{A}$ to $\mathbf{B}$.

Dimensions, Units, and Notation

Typographical conventions

Mathematical and Symbol Notation

Electromagnetics mixes scalars, vectors, unit vectors and phasors in the same equation. A fixed set of typographical rules tells them apart at a glance — the same rules used throughout this course (Ulaby, Fundamentals of Applied Electromagnetics).

Scalars

Medium-weight italic

Scalar quantities carry magnitude only, e.g. $C$ for capacitance, $V$ for voltage, $\rho_{\mathrm{v}}$ for volume charge density.

$$C, \quad R, \quad V, \quad \rho_{\mathrm{v}}$$
Units

Medium-weight roman

Units are upright (roman), never italic: $\mathrm{V/m}$ for volts per meter, $\mathrm{C/m^2}$ for coulombs per square meter.

$$\mathrm{V/m}, \quad \mathrm{C/m^2}, \quad \mathrm{A/m}$$
Vectors

Boldface roman

Vector quantities are boldface roman: $\mathbf{E}$ is the electric field vector, $E = |\mathbf{E}|$ its magnitude.

$$\mathbf{E}, \quad \mathbf{H}, \quad \mathbf{D}, \quad \mathbf{B}, \quad \mathbf{J}$$
Unit vectors

Boldface with circumflex

A unit vector has magnitude 1 and marks a direction; it carries a circumflex (hat): $\mathbf{A} = \boldsymbol{\hat{a}}\,A$.

$$\hat{\mathbf{x}}, \quad \hat{\mathbf{y}}, \quad \hat{\mathbf{z}}, \quad \hat{\mathbf{r}}, \quad \boldsymbol{\hat{\phi}}, \quad \hat{\mathbf{R}}, \quad \boldsymbol{\hat{\theta}}$$
Phasors

Tilde over the letter

$\widetilde{E}$ is the phasor of the sinusoidal scalar $E(t)$ and $\widetilde{\mathbf{E}}$ the phasor of the vector $\mathbf{E}(t)$: $\mathbf{E}(t) = \mathfrak{Re}[\widetilde{\mathbf{E}}\,e^{j\omega t}]$.

$$\widetilde{\mathbf{E}}(x,y,z), \quad \widetilde{\mathbf{H}}(x,y,z), \quad \widetilde{\mathbf{J}}(x,y,z)$$
Put together

One equation shows every convention. The field of a point charge is a vector (bold) built from a scalar magnitude (italic) and a unit vector (hat), and its unit is written upright:

$$\mathbf{E} = \hat{\mathbf{R}}\,\frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R^2} \quad (\mathrm{V/m}), \qquad \widetilde{\mathbf{E}}(z) = \hat{\mathbf{x}}\,E_0\,e^{-jkz}$$

Unit algebra

Interactive Simulation 2 · Dimensional Analysis Lab

Every valid equation must have the same dimensions on both sides. Build a product of quantities with exponents and the lab reduces it to fundamental units (kg, m, s, C) and names the result.

Result in fundamental units

Try the preset $1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}$: the result is a velocity — Maxwell's first hint that light is an electromagnetic wave. $\sqrt{\mu_0/\varepsilon_0}$ comes out in ohms: the intrinsic impedance of free space, $\eta_0 \approx 377\ \Omega$.

Review of Phasors

In a linear system driven by a sinusoid, every voltage, current and field oscillates at the same angular frequency $\omega$. Only amplitudes and phases differ — and a single complex number, the phasor, stores both. Differential equations in $t$ become algebraic equations.

Definition
$$z(t) = \mathfrak{Re}\big[\widetilde{Z}\,e^{j\omega t}\big]$$

$z(t) = A\cos(\omega t + \phi) \ \leftrightarrow\ \widetilde{Z} = Ae^{j\phi}$. The phasor is time-independent.

Calculus becomes algebra
$$\frac{dz}{dt} \leftrightarrow j\omega\widetilde{Z}, \quad \int z\,dt \leftrightarrow \frac{\widetilde{Z}}{j\omega}$$

Multiplying by $j$ is a $+90^\circ$ phase shift; dividing by $j$ is $-90^\circ$.

Waves too
$$A\cos(\omega t \mp \beta x) \leftrightarrow Ae^{\mp j\beta x}$$

A position-dependent phase $e^{-j\beta x}$ describes a wave travelling in $+x$.

Analytical method

Five-Step Solution of the RC Equation

Kirchhoff's voltage law around the loop gives an integro-differential equation. The phasor method turns it into one line of algebra.

$$R\,i(t) + \frac{1}{C}\int i(t)\,dt = v_s(t) = V_0\sin(\omega t + \phi_0)$$
Step 11

Adopt a cosine reference

Write every source as a cosine using $\sin x = \cos(x - \pi/2)$:

$$v_s(t) = V_0\cos\!\left(\omega t + \phi_0 - \tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$$
Step 22

Express as phasors

Each quantity becomes $z(t) = \mathfrak{Re}[\widetilde{Z}e^{j\omega t}]$:

$$\widetilde{V}_s = V_0\,e^{j(\phi_0 - \pi/2)}$$
Step 33

Recast the equation

Replace $d/dt \rightarrow j\omega$ and $\int dt \rightarrow 1/j\omega$:

$$\widetilde{I}\left(R + \frac{1}{j\omega C}\right) = \widetilde{V}_s$$
Step 44

Solve the algebra

With $\phi_1 = \tan^{-1}(\omega RC)$:

$$\widetilde{I} = \frac{V_0\,\omega C}{\sqrt{1+\omega^2R^2C^2}}\,e^{j(\phi_0 - \phi_1)}$$
Step 55

Return to time domain

Multiply by $e^{j\omega t}$ and take the real part:

$$\begin{aligned} i(t) &= \frac{V_0\,\omega C}{\sqrt{1+\omega^2R^2C^2}} \\ &\quad\times\cos(\omega t + \phi_0 - \phi_1) \end{aligned}$$

Checks: for $\omega RC \gg 1$ the capacitor is almost a short, $\phi_1 \to 90^\circ$, and $i(t)$ is in phase with $v_s(t)$ — a pure resistor. For $\omega RC \ll 1$, $\phi_1 \to 0$ and the current leads the source by $90^\circ$ — a pure capacitor. Test both limits with the simulation.

Phasors of Travelling Waves

The phasor $Ae^{-j\beta x}$ has a phase that falls steadily with distance. Multiplied by $e^{j\omega t}$ it becomes a wave moving in $+x$; with $e^{+j\beta x}$ it moves in $-x$. The small dials show the phasor (frozen at $t = 0$) at several positions.

Rule: the sign in front of $j\beta x$ is opposite to the direction of travel.
